11. **Task [Extended] - Evidence base for the questionnaire design** (v0.6.9): Ran a 13-agent research workflow over questionnaire and settings-UI design, adjudicated its findings against the primary sources, and wrote the reference the candidate designs are judged against<br>
    **Result**: Workflow `wf_9a43fc71-ec9` produced `docs/research-settings-ui.md`, 6,398 words: a 20-row claim/verdict table, 53 imperative rules with sources and costs, prior art from menuconfig, nconfig, Kconfiglib, debconf, VisiData, btop and GOV.UK, 16 traps, and a closing section naming the four gaps nobody has measured. The point of the exercise was that the design conversation had been running on folklore. Ten of the twenty claims fail against their own primaries: choice overload (Scheibehenne 2010, D = 0.02, N = 5,036, and Chernev 2015's own no-moderator model agreeing at p = .48), Miller's 7 plus or minus 2 (his paper calls it "a pernicious, Pythagorean coincidence" and measures absolute judgment, not visible lists), Hick's law (Liu, CHI 2020: "Hick's law speaks against, not for, the popular principle that 'less is better'"), and progress indicators (meta-analysis null, 1.56x dropoff odds for the slow-then-fast shape this survey produces). Decisively for the work in flight, no study in the entire choice-overload literature ever manipulated visibility - every arm showed every option - so hiding alternatives behind a dropdown has no evidential support at all, and named sections have a direct null (Smyth 2006 isolated headings from spacing: 64.6% against 66.8%, p = .538) while the spacing carries the effect. The finding that reverses an earlier recommendation is Luguri and Strahilevitz: presenting the same content less prominently doubled acceptance from 14.8% to 30.1%, so dimming untouched defaults is hiding them, not styling them, and the cheap-verification result (Eggs and Jackle: task difficulty predicts confirming a false preload, motivation does not) says the untouched rows are exactly the ones that must stay legible. The evidence therefore favours a dense, always-visible, undimmed list over both the accordion and the two-tier brightness encoding. `docs/README.md` indexes the file.
